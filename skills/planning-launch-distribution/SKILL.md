# Planning Launch Distribution

Use a verified product story to help the right people find, understand, and try the release. Do not publish, post, send messages, or schedule external content without user authorization.

## Workflow

1. Identify audience, launch objective, available proof, release readiness, primary link and CTA. If a launch brief or showcase exists, reuse its verified facts; otherwise inspect the project before claiming functionality.
2. Select only channels where the intended audience actually participates. Distinguish GitHub release/readme updates, a portfolio or product page, LinkedIn, and a relevant community. Check current posting requirements and moderation rules on the selected platforms before recommending timing or format.
3. Build a small asset matrix: channel, audience angle, opening line, proof (short video, screenshot, demo or repository), CTA, destination, owner, and publication order. Adapt each post to its context; don't paste the same copy into every channel.
4. Draft launch copy that leads with a concrete problem or result, demonstrates an actual product moment, states availability and limitations honestly, and makes the next action explicit. Credit collaborators and original projects where relevant. Avoid invented popularity, fake scarcity, or blanket claims of being production-ready.
5. Plan a short follow-up: answer installation questions, gather reproducible reports, and observe available signals such as qualified clicks, installs, stars, demo completions, or contributor interest. Never infer installs or business impact from impressions alone.
6. Deliver ready-to-review drafts, link and asset checklist, release sequence, reply plan, and lightweight feedback log. Mark links and platform limits requiring final verification. Publish only when authorized.
