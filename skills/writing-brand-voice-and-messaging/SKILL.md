# Writing Brand Voice and Messaging

## Core principle
Voice is the brand's durable verbal character; tone adapts that voice to audience state and context. Build a reusable messaging system before optimizing any individual campaign or landing page.

## Voice and messaging workflow
1. Start from approved brand strategy. Capture audience tensions, positioning, value proposition, proof, personality, and claims the brand can actually support.
2. Define the messaging hierarchy: core promise, supporting pillars, proof points, differentiators, descriptors, and recurring terminology.
3. Write voice traits as operational contrasts. Instead of “friendly”, define something such as “plainspoken, not casual to the point of vagueness” and show what that changes in actual sentences.
4. Separate voice from tone. Keep the voice recognizable while adapting directness, warmth, urgency, humor, and detail to the reader's situation and emotional state.
5. Define terminology. Record preferred product/category words, capitalization, names, phrases to avoid, jargon rules, and how technical concepts should be explained.
6. Create message examples across representative contexts: brand introduction, product description, support or error state, announcement, social caption, and another channel central to the brand.
7. Test claims against evidence. Remove vague superiority, invented purpose, and promises the product or organization cannot substantiate.
8. Create a concise do/don't set for voice and tone, then connect it to `building-brand-guidelines` so verbal and visual governance share one source of truth.

## Boundary with conversion copy
This method defines the durable verbal system. It does not optimize a landing page, email, ad, or CTA for conversion. When a future conversion-copy method is available, use the brand voice and messaging produced here as an input to that method.

## Quality checks
The system should:
- sound recognizable across different channels without repeating the same sentence patterns;
- distinguish stable voice from situational tone;
- make abstract traits observable through examples;
- preserve clarity over cleverness;
- support localization without depending on untranslatable wordplay;
- keep claims tied to evidence.

## Verification
Give the guidance and the same content brief to two writers. If their outputs can both feel on-brand while differing naturally in wording, the system is providing useful constraints rather than a script.
